# Probes of PRIMER.exe, night of 2026-10-20 to 21

Six open questions of the proposal (§6.4) were probed with DOSBox-X, headless, with
`PAUSE` 3 to 4 and at most 3 emulators (a fuzzing campaign was using 16 cores).
Each section lists what was run, where the recordings are, what was seen, and a
draft paragraph for `tests/legacy/README.md`. Nothing in the README, the tools or the
earlier recordings was changed.

Conventions:
- `probe_keys.sh`, `print_report.sh`, `find_pair_limits.sh`: the tools of
  `tools/`, run with `PRIMER_EXE=.../examples/PRIMER.exe`. The inputs came from a
  copy of `tests/legacy` taken from git `HEAD` (see "What went wrong").
- `NAME.screens.txt`: the screens from `tools/screen_text.py`, with the splash
  screens (and the licence serial number) taken out by the new
  `tools/strip_splash.py`.
- `NAME.PRN`: the file PRIMER.exe wrote with Print to Disk (`REPORT`), kept as
  written (DOS line ends, code page 437).
- "predicted": the outcome was written down before the recording, from
  primerpicker's CLI (`find-pair`, `homology`) or from a model stated in the
  section.
- Several runs lost keys or froze (DOSBox-X stopped early). They were rerun and
  are not kept. Each directory's `README.txt` lists the runs that are kept.

Key helpers (shell, used with `probe_keys.sh`):

    # Find Pair with chosen offsets, then print the list to disk
    # (CFG=1: PD2.CFG listed before the molecule, one more down)
    KEYS="space down down down enter enter [down] down down enter , , enter
          TA enter OA[enter] enter enter TB enter OB[enter] enter enter enter enter
          , , , , space , , p , enter , down down enter , r e p o r t enter , ,"

(digits are typed one by one, `1 5 0`; a 2-digit offset needs Enter, a 3-digit
one moves on by itself). The exact key lines are in the `run.conf` lines quoted in
each `README.txt`.

- target A of 0, or > L: "Must enter value for this field";

  B's length 20 would have let it through: it is primer A's length that counts);
- target B > L: "Must enter value for this field" (checked after primer A: 650/700
  gives one box, and target A was wrong too);
- target B < primer B's length (10 and 19 for 20 bases; 20 and 21 are accepted):
  "Primer too long to fit within molecule.". A primer B ending at its target would
  start before base 1. Predicted from the primer A rule before the 19/20/21 runs.
  primerpicker accepts targets B 10 and 19 and cuts the window;
- target B = L is accepted (a window of one position, "End: Target + 0");
  windows are cut at the molecule's end as before.

**Not settled:** the field the cursor lands on after a box on target B; whether
primer B's own length field is the one used (both lengths were 20 in the target B
runs).

## 2. Find Pair report of more than 20 pairs ("Print best: [N]")

**Recordings:** `reports/find_pair_best/`. `search/TFP.GB` with targets 150 and 400
and built-in settings (30 pairs). `pairs/TEST600.GB` with targets 150 and 400 and
`config/PD2_CRITERIA.CFG` (1000 pairs, the cap).

**Command:**

    PRINT_KEYS=", , p , , D D enter , , down down enter , , r e p o r t enter , , , ," \
        print_report.sh WORKDIR find_pair search/TFP.GB 150 400

(for 4 digits: `", p , , 1 , 0 , 0 , 0 , , down down enter , r e p o r t enter , ,"`,
with no Enter after them; with CFG=config/PD2_CRITERIA.CFG for TEST600).

**Observed.** The box reads "30 Pairs found.  Print best: [20  ]" (and "1000 Pairs
found.  Print best: [20  ]"). The first digit typed clears the field ("[3?  ]",
where `?` is the cursor). The field holds 4 digits, and a fourth digit completes it
by itself: "[100?]" then the Print Control menu with no Enter. An Enter typed after
it selects the first item of Print Control, and nothing is written. 1, 2 or 3 digits
need Enter.

| pairs | typed N | report |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 25 | the best 25 rows, in the order of the list |
| 30 | 30 | the 30 rows |
| 30 | 50 | the 30 rows (same file as N = 30, but the date line) |
| 30 | 9999 | "[999 ]" shown, then the 30 rows (same file) |
| 30 | 0 | the box closes, no Print Control, nothing written; the list stays |
| 1000 | 100 | the best 100 rows |
| 1000 | 1000 | the 1000 rows (70916 bytes) |

None of the reports has a page break or a form feed (no byte 0C), and the header is
printed once. Rows 21 to N are formatted as rows 1 to 20. The 25, 30, 50 and 9999
files are the 20-row report with the next rows appended. Their rows are
primerpicker's list, in its order (`find-pair` without `--report`). The first 100
rows of both TEST600 files are primerpicker's first 100.

**The 1000-row report and primerpicker differ by one pair.** PRIMER.exe lists
148/421 (row 578) and leaves out 71/422, which is primerpicker's row 1000 (the
worst-ranked pair). primerpicker ranks the first 1000 pairs it generates. To list
every pair, primerpicker was run once per position of the B window (offsets 100,0,
targets 150 and 400 to 500), which gives 1034 pairs. In generation order (primer A,
then primer B, by position), 148/421 is pair number 1001.
- "Rank the first 1001 pairs generated, keep the best 1000" gives the recorded file
  exactly, rows and order. The rows are primerpicker's list with 71/422 taken out
  and 148/421 put in at row 578. No other number of pairs from 1001 to 1034 gives
  the recorded set.
- "Rank the first 1000" (primerpicker v1, README "Limits on primers and pairs")
  differs by this one pair. "Rank all 1034, keep 1000" differs by 66 pairs.
  Generating primer B first differs by 68 pairs.
- 71/422 is a pair for PRIMER.exe. Find Pair with the one-position windows 71 and
  422 (`find_pair_limits.sh ... TEST600.GB 71 0 422 0`, PD2_CRITERIA.CFG) shows
  "Primer A: 1", "Primer B: 1", "Pairs: 1"
  (`find_pair_trun/TEST600_71_0_422_0_CRITERIA.screens.txt`).

**Prediction test.** A second case was designed where the three models disagree in the
best 20. The input `find_pair_trun/PD2_CRITERIA_W10131.CFG` is PD2_CRITERIA.CFG with
the weights GC 1, Tm 0, 3' dimers 3, any dimers 1, false priming 1. With it,
TEST600 with target A 260 and target B 400 (offsets 100) has 1013 pairs, and the 1001st generated is
260/450. The models were scored with the README score from primerpicker's per-pair
values. This scoring reproduces the 1000-row file above row for row, with that
file's weights (2, 1, 3, 1, 4). The predictions for the best 20:
- first 1000 (primerpicker): no 260/450;
- first 1001: 260/450 at row 7, and no 260/451;
- all 1013: 260/450 at row 7 and 260/451 at row 8.

PRIMER.exe ("Pairs:  1000 possible"; `find_pair_trun/TEST600_260_400_CRITERIA_W10131.PRN`)
prints the "first 1001" list exactly: 242/413, 245/413, 172/427, 172/432, 173/413,
260/449, **260/450**, 173/457, ..., 238/414.

**Rule** (2 searches): when the pair count reaches the cap, the program has
generated 1001 pairs (primer A, then primer B, by position). It ranks them and keeps
the best 1000. Whether it stops there or goes on (the hang on TLONG.GB, README) is
not known. Both searches here ended normally.

## 3. A complement-strand primer in the Uniqueness and False Priming reports

**Recordings:** `homology_complement/`. `homology/TREP2.GB` (primer at 400,
complement strand: TGTATCCAGGTAATGCAAGG, sites already in `homology/homology.tsv`)
and `homology_complement/TCOMP.GB`. TCOMP.GB is an input written for these probes,
not a recording:

    plant_sites.py TCOMP 2110 0.5,0.5,0.5,0.5 250 681:12:200 TTCACCGGTTAA@450 689:10:900 CGGTTAAGGGG@300

It is 1000 bases with copies of parts of the complement-strand primer at 700
(ACAATTCACCGGTTAAGGGG, bases 681 to 700). The copies are on the top strand (200, 900)
and reverse complemented (300, 450).

**Command** (the keys of `homology_screen.sh` for a complement primer, then Print):

    probe_keys.sh WORKDIR NAME "space enter enter down down enter , , enter P P P enter
        y enter enter enter s , , right right enter , , u|f , , right , , enter , ,
        down down enter , , r e p o r t enter , , , ," MOLECULE

**Observed.** Reports were written for TREP2 at 400 (u, f), TCOMP at 700 (u, f) and
TCOMP at 708 (u). For TREP2 at 405, only the screen was kept (the file name was
lost while typing). Compared with a top-strand report:
- the position line reads `                  Bp pos:   400        Complementary strand`
  (the position in 6 columns, 8 spaces, "Complementary strand"; the top-strand
  report has the position only);
- the map marks the primer `<P` instead of `P>`. In the report, `P` is in column
  P / s - 1 (P = the position typed, the 5' end of the primer, on the top-strand
  scale; s = L / 73 + 1), the column of `P` in `P>`, and `<` is just left of it. TREP2 at
  400 (s = 9): `<` at 42, `P` at 43. TCOMP at 700 and at 708 (s = 14): `<` at 48, `P` at
  49 in both. The model "`<` at (P - 19) / s, the primer's 3' end" gives 49 for 708
  and is contradicted. "`P` at P / s - 1" was predicted before the 708 recording;
- the sites, their marks (`>` for a row without "(C)", `<` for "(C)", in column
  3 + (Bp Pos - 1) / s), the "(C)" labels and the positions follow the rules of
  "Uniqueness and false priming", applied to the primer's sequence as written 5'
  to 3'. A site is "(C)" when that sequence occurs on the complement strand. For
  TCOMP at 700, the top-strand copy at 200 (bases 681 to 692) is listed as
  `CCGGTTAAGGGG 211 (C)`, and the reverse-complement copy planted at 300 as
  `CACCGGTTAAGGGG 297` (no "(C)"). The position is the base facing the 5'-most
  matching base: 211 = 200 + (692 - 681), the matching bases being primer offsets 8 to
  19;
- the rest (title, criteria, rows, Tm values) is as in the top-strand reports.
On the screen (not the report), the map has `<P` with `P` in screen column
4 + (P - 1) / s: TREP2 400 and 405 both 48, TCOMP 700 at 53 and 708 at 54. The report
and screen formulas differ (the top-strand screen map was not checked). Only these
4 screens were checked.

(The Primer List shows such a primer as `405(C)`.)

Near the molecule's start (`homology_complement/TCSTART.GB`, an input:
`plant_sites.py TCSTART 2111 0.5,0.5,0.5,0.5 250 1:12:500 9:12:700`, 1000 bases,
s = 14), Uniqueness reports of complement primers at 20 and 28 show `<P` in
columns 0 and 1 in both (`TCSTART_20c_u.PRN`, `TCSTART_28c_u.PRN`). For 20,
P / s - 1 = 0 would put `<` in column -1. So `<P` starts in column
max(0, P / s - 2), as `P>` starts in max(0, P / s - 1) for a top-strand primer.

**Not settled:** a complement primer with "Primer is unique" or "No false priming
found".

recordings. Built-in defaults were written with `tools/make_pd2_cfg.py`
(`PD2_BASE.CFG`), then bytes 0-3 / 47-54 were set to:
- float32 1.0 / float64 1.0: `PD2_F1_D1.CFG`;
- float32 50.0 / float64 50.0: `PD2_F50_D50.CFG`;
- FF x 4 / FF x 8: `PD2_FF.CFG`;

  recorded with PD2_BASE.CFG:
  - One Primer: F1_D1, F50_D50, FF, I10;
  - Find Pair: F1_D1, F50_D50, FF, I10;
  - Uniqueness: F1_D1, F50_D50, FF, I10.

  The BASE reports themselves equal primerpicker's built-in reports (and BASE's
  Find Pair report equals `reports/FINDPAIR.PRN`).
- The Analyze Amplified Product screen (product size 350, melting temperature 77 °C,
  annealing temperature 52 °C) is the same for BASE, F1_D1, F50_D50 and FF.
- Was the file loaded at all? The variants above hold built-in values, so a file
  ignored as invalid would give the same results. So the same bytes were written
  into PD2_RELAXED.CFG (RELAXED_F1_D1, RELAXED_F50_D50, RELAXED_FF, RELAXED_I10). Their One Primer

  (zero bytes there).
- With 01 02 03 04 in bytes 0-3, the System Defaults screens show nothing new
  (`config_header/`, 2026-10-20).

**Conclusion (for the observations made):** PRIMER.exe reads bytes 0-3 and 47-54,
writes them back unchanged, and no recorded output depends on them. No settings
field writes them: the save of every Defaults field in (a), and of the Criteria,
Weights and runs fields earlier (`config/`, `config_header/`), leaves them zero.
They look unused (padding, or a field of a later or earlier version), but that is
not proven. Not tried: Match Active Primer, Multi Primer, the restriction enzyme
screens, printing to a printer.

**Region 3 (282 pairs where primerpicker finds 300): Find Pair applies the A:B
adjacent-bases criterion.** Of primerpicker's 300 pairs, exactly 18 have A:B "Any adj
homol bases" (from `primerpicker pair`) of 7, the criterion. 300 - 18 = 282. This is
the rule already known for Match Active Primer ("fewer A:B adjacent pairs than the
criterion"). No pair of any other recorded Find Pair list (TEST600, TFP, TFP2, TLIN,
TLONG 200/800, TRUN regions 0-2, TEST600 with WEIGHTS35142 and with CRITERIA)
reaches its criterion, so the rule changes none of them. To test it, the criterion
was changed so that the models disagree. These were predicted before the recording:

| settings | targets | primerpicker | A:B rule (predicted) | PRIMER.exe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PD2_RUNS4_ADJ6 | 1280/1380 | 22, 20, 203 | 173 | 22, 20, **173** |
| PD2_RUNS4_ADJ8 | 1280/1380 | 22, 27, 300 | 300 | 22, 27, **300** |
| PD2_RUNS4_ADJ5 | 880/980 | 19, 29, 194 | 160 | 19, 29, **160** |

(primer A, primer B, pairs). ADJ8 rules out a fixed limit of 7. The ADJ5 report
(best 20) is primerpicker's list without the pairs whose A:B adjacent bases reach 5,
in the same order (checked row by row: primerpicker's own report differs in 4 rows).
The ADJ6 report equals primerpicker's (its best 20 are not affected).

**Region 4 (24 primers B and 117 pairs where primerpicker finds 25 and 118): primer
B at 1867 (ATCTGTGGGACCGCCTGCTT) is not a possible primer for PRIMER.exe.**
primerpicker's 25 primers B include 3 with a single pair (1784, 1821, 1867, each
with A 1648 or 1607). Find Pair with target A 1680 (offset 100) and a one-position B
window: 1784 and 1821 give "Primer B: 1", "Pairs: 1"; 1867 gives "No Matching
Primers Found". With the window 1867 to 1870 (offset 3), primerpicker predicts
2 primers B and 7 pairs; PRIMER.exe shows "Primer B: 1 possible primers", "Pairs: 6
possible". So 1867 is left out as a primer, not as a pair. **The cause is a
hairpin.** The One Primer report of ATCTGTGGGACCGCCTGCTT with PD2_RUNS4.CFG
(`print_report.sh inspect`, `find_pair_trun/INSPECT_ATCTGTGGGACCGCCTGCTT_RUNS4.PRN`)
equals primerpicker's (`analyze --report`) but for one line:

    No Hairpins      Energy cutoff  0.0 kcal              --           NO

primerpicker writes YES ("no hairpins found"). The Hairpins screen
(`tools/inspect_primer.sh`, built-in settings,
`find_pair_trun/INSPECT_SCREENS_ATCTGTGGGACCGCCTGCTT.screens.txt`) shows:

    Hairpin Energy = -1.0 kcals

    5'-ATCTGTGGGAC
           :::::  )
      3'-TTCGTCCGC

The stem pairs primer bases 5-9 (GTGGG) with bases 18-14 (CGTCC): G:C, T.G, G.T,
G:C, G:C, with a loop of 4 (ACCG, bases 10-13). It holds a **tandem T.G/G.T step**,
which the README's hairpin model excludes ("stems do not extend through a tandem
T.G/G.T step (no recorded stem contains one)"). This is the first recorded stem with
one, and the reason primerpicker misses the hairpin. With the README model (loop of
4: 5.9; GG/CC: 2.9; shown energy truncated toward zero), the three steps
G:C→T.G, T.G→G.T and G.T→G:C add up to between 4.0 and 4.1 kcal (from
-1.1 < 5.9 - 2.9 - x <= -1.0). This is one equation for the tandem step. It was
not checked further.

So region 4 is explained: PRIMER.exe's Find Pair applies the hairpin criterion with
this hairpin, so 1867 is not a possible primer B (24 primers, 117 pairs).

Two primers designed with a tandem G.T step (`hairpin_tandem_gt/`, Direct Entry,
`tools/inspect_primer.sh`):
- ACCTGGCAAAAGCTGGGTTA, with the same order as 1867 (T.G then G.T: stem A:T C:G
  C:G T.G G.T G:C C:G, loop AAAA): "Hairpin Energy = -6.1 kcals", 7 pairs drawn.
  primerpicker gives -0.1 (another hairpin), which confirms that it misses this
  kind of stem;
- ACCGTGGAAAACCGTGGTTA, with the other order (G.T then T.G: A:T C:G C:G G.T T.G
  G:C G:C): "Hairpin Energy = -5.5 kcals", 7 pairs drawn. primerpicker gives
  -5.5 too (its CLI does not show the stem).

**TEST600 with PD2_CRITERIA.CFG, pair 71/422** (see 2.): found by PRIMER.exe in a
one-position search, so it is not left out by a pair rule. It is left out of the
1000-pair report by the cap: the first 1001 pairs are ranked and 1000 kept.
